Six tools I built on my own. Five do one job each, and the sixth brings them together.

  • Influencer ranking Scores and ranks influencer profiles on normalized engagement, then tracks how campaigns perform.
  • Brand research Studies a brand’s web presence and asks several AI models the same question, keeping what they agree on.
  • Social reporting Brings social metrics from several platforms into one dashboard, with competitor benchmarks and exportable reports.
  • Content calendar Plans, publishes and measures social content across platforms.
  • Media relations A database of publishers and journalists for planning outreach.
  • One platform A single login, database and dashboard that brings the tools together, with a shared task queue behind them.

What I did

I joined Grapes Worldwide, a digital and social media agency, as an AI Engineer Intern on the CPO track. The brief was to take work the teams were doing by hand and turn it into tools they could run themselves.

I designed and built six of those tools end to end, on my own: the data collection, the backend, the scoring or AI logic, and the dashboard people actually use.

The parts I’m proudest of

  • Influence Model 2.0. A feature-engineered ranking system that scores 500+ influencer profiles on normalized engagement metrics. It cut manual campaign evaluation by about 3 hours a day.
  • Asking several AI models, not one. A consensus-based evaluation framework that puts the same question to more than one model and keeps what they agree on. It was used across 10+ enterprise brands.
  • Pipelines that don’t repeat themselves. Python ETL and ingestion pipelines with a caching layer that cut redundant API requests by about 70%.

Working with a team

I also worked with teammates on two market-research tools, collecting and analysing structured and unstructured data to produce business intelligence reports.
